# Building YTU Technologies

The founder story of Leul Ali

My name is Leul Ali, also known as Leul YTU. I grew up in Hawassa, Ethiopia, and I am building YTU Technologies in Reston, Virginia. YTU stands for Your Technology Unlocked. It expresses what I want my work to do: help people use technology to solve problems in their lives and businesses.

My path has included a difficult childhood, an interrupted education, a move to a new country, and the work of supporting myself while continuing to learn. Along the way, I have worked in digital services, retail, security operations, and data-center environments. Those experiences are part of the background to YTU. They have given me practical problems to think about alongside the larger questions that first drew me toward technology.

## Growing up in Hawassa

I grew up with limited resources. Poverty was a real part of my childhood, and I also experienced bullying and abuse. These are difficult parts of my history. I can acknowledge them without sharing every private detail or allowing them to become the only way people understand my life.

Financial hardship affects the choices a person can make. Education and opportunities to develop a skill can require resources that are difficult to obtain. My interest in technology developed within those constraints. I wanted to learn, build things, and create a future with more possibilities than the circumstances around me seemed to offer.

Hawassa remains an important part of who I am. My Ethiopian background belongs in the story of the company I am building. So does the fact that progress has required patience, learning, and help along the way. I am still early in that process. When I talk about wanting technology to create opportunities, I am speaking from a life in which access to opportunity has mattered deeply.

## Learning by building

As my interest in coding grew, I began working across different kinds of digital projects. My experience has included full-stack development, websites, applications, AI assistants, digital marketing, graphic design, and video editing. Working across these areas gave me several ways to approach a problem, from how a system functions to how someone understands and uses it.

I have pursued ambitious ideas as well as practical work. My early experiments included AI applications and software related to autonomous motorcycles. Some ideas have remained experiments. Others have developed into projects that I continue to work on under YTU. Building them has been part of my education, alongside university study and continued technical learning.

I want to keep that connection between curiosity and useful work. A technical idea becomes more interesting to me when I can explain who it might help and what problem it could address. That question is also a way to challenge my own assumptions. A concept may look promising on a screen while still needing substantial work before someone can rely on it.

## An education that is still important to me

I studied information technology at Hawassa University. My university education was interrupted, and continuing toward a bachelor's degree has remained an important goal. Moving between education systems has brought further challenges, including obtaining records, understanding transfer evaluations, and finding an affordable route to completion.

That part of my story is still unfinished. I have had to work through the difference between what I studied, how an institution evaluates it, and what I must do next. Financial constraints have also affected the timing of my plans. Continuing an education can involve months of administrative work as well as the learning itself.

Throughout that process, I have continued developing technical skills and building projects. My work has involved Python, data tools, automation, and an increasing interest in the infrastructure that supports software. I value formal education, and I want to complete it. At the same time, I have kept looking for ways to gain experience and make progress with the opportunities available to me.

## Starting again in the United States

I arrived in the United States in August 2025. The move brought the possibility of continuing my education and building a career in technology, along with the immediate responsibility of establishing a life in a new country.

I have had to balance work, education plans, and the development of my own projects. Some of the work I took on was outside the technical career I wanted to pursue. It was part of supporting myself and finding a way forward. Retail work, in particular, became relevant to my interest in how people find information and make decisions while shopping.

I also began gaining experience in data-center environments. That work brought me closer to the physical infrastructure behind the services people use every day. My background in technology gave me a reason to keep learning about it and to look for opportunities to move toward more hands-on technical operations.

## What operational work added

My experience has included security and control-room work at data centers, followed by work in data-center operations. In control-room roles, my responsibilities included monitoring alarms, documenting incidents, coordinating handoffs, and escalating issues through the appropriate channels. These were environments where people needed clear information and consistent follow-through.

The experience connects directly to my interest in operational software. A shift change, a missing detail, or information spread across several places can create work for the next person. Software can help organize that information, but it has to fit the way people actually do their jobs. Understanding the workflow is part of understanding the technical problem.

An internal operations tool is one of the practical projects in YTU's story. It sits alongside my broader AI interests because improving an everyday process is meaningful work. I want to keep learning from the people who use a system and from the points where their work becomes harder than it needs to be.

## The company I am building

YTU Technologies brings these interests together around a straightforward approach: Listen. Understand. Unlock. I want to begin with a person's problem, understand the current process and its constraints, and then work out what technology could usefully change.

That can mean a focused software project for a business. It can also mean exploring a more ambitious technical idea over time. The scope should come from the problem and the evidence available. For a business owner, a clearer intake process or a better way to organize requests may be a useful place to begin. My aim is to build tools that people can understand and control as they become part of their work.

YTU AI is one part of that effort. It is available as a public beta and remains under development. My work has explored AI assistants and ways of helping people interact with software and information. Earlier experiments also included locally running AI vision. These projects give me concrete systems to improve while I continue learning what people need and where the technology falls short.

Veriqa is a separate shopping-intelligence prototype. It explores how product scanning could help shoppers understand alternatives, value, and suitability, including in grocery shopping. It connects my interest in AI with practical questions about how people make sense of product information and choices. The prototype is a way to explore that problem. Its usefulness in a store and any commercial benefit still need to be demonstrated through testing and actual use.

I have also continued exploring autonomy, including software experiments related to self-driving motorcycles. This remains early-stage research. Simulation and evaluation are important parts of that work, and any real-world use would require extensive engineering and safety validation. There is substantial engineering work ahead.

Across these projects, I want to be clear about what exists today and what I hope to build. A beta, a prototype, and a research idea each have a place in a young company. Describing them accurately helps people understand the work and gives us a sound basis for discussing what should happen next.

## Why I am building now

My long-term interests extend to space technology and humanity's future beyond Earth. I want YTU to develop the ability to contribute to difficult technical problems over time. Space is a long-term ambition, and the work in front of me today is to build useful software, develop my skills, and establish a company people can trust.

There are challenges I am still working through. Completing my education, developing reliable products, and turning ideas into sustainable work all require effort. I want to be open about being at this stage. The story of YTU is being written through the decisions I make and the work I do now.

I am building because I believe technology can make life easier, create opportunities, and help people. My experiences in Ethiopia and the United States have given that belief a personal meaning. I want the company to grow through solving real problems and learning from the people affected by them.
